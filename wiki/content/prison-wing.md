Every [trader outpost](trader-outposts.md) has a **lockup**: a six-cell prison block with a shared yard, run by a front-desk warden and two prison guards. You can watch the yard through heavy glass from the concourse, ride the hangar elevator straight to the **Lockup** floor to visit or hand in a live [bounty catch](bounty-hunting.md), or end up inside it yourself when an arrest carries real time.

## Finding the lockup

Pick **Lockup** on any hangar elevator panel at Waystation Halcyon, Quartermain Depot or the Undertow Exchange. Anyone can use the stop, and dragged prisoners ride the car with you.

The elevator opens into the **front room**. Like the arrivals lobby, it sits outside the weapons checkpoint, so carrying a holstered gun up from your berth to deliver a bounty is legal, though [outpost security](trader-outposts.md#outpost-security) still enforces the law if you start a fight.

- **The front desk.** A warden keeps the booth at each outpost: **Vance** at Halcyon, **Keller** at Quartermain and **Morrow** at the Undertow.
- **Property lockers.** Four personal security lockers stand in the front room for inmates' gear.
- **Visitor glass.** Heavy reinforced glass looks straight into the yard and cell block. You can see and talk through it, and nothing passes through it, gunfire included.
- **Processing.** A two-door sally port between the front room and the yard. Only one of its doors opens at a time. Everyone booked in, released, or handed in for a bounty goes through it.

Behind Processing sit six cells (each with a bunk, chair and toilet), the mess tables, basketball hoop, bookcase, water cooler, washing machine, mop bucket and yard cleanbot, with the guards' office and two **serving hatches** along the divider.

## Ending up inside

Outpost officers bring you to the lockup after an arrest when a quick station fine won't cover it:

| Stars | When you go to the lockup | Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 star | Your crew's ship accounts can't cover the fine | 3 minutes |
| 2 stars | Your crew's ship accounts can't cover the fine | 6 minutes |
| 3 stars | Taken alive at Halcyon or Quartermain | 10 minutes |
| 4 stars | Taken alive at Halcyon or Quartermain | 15 minutes |
| 5 stars | Taken alive at Halcyon or Quartermain | 20 minutes |

At 1 or 2 stars, if your crew can pay the fine right away, you serve 30 or 60 seconds in the security station cell instead. If you are sent to the lockup for an unpaid fine, the outpost tries the fine again every 30 seconds against your crew's ship accounts; as soon as a crewmate deposits enough to cover it, your sentence ends. From 3 stars up there is no fine and no bail, and at the Undertow Exchange officers shoot to kill from 3 stars up instead of taking prisoners.

After logging any crime weapons at the security desk, the officer rides the lift with you to the Lockup floor, strips your gear into one of the front-room **property lockers**, dresses you in an orange prison jumpsuit and shoes, walks you into Processing, takes the cuffs off and steps out. Once the outer door shuts, the inner door opens to the yard and your ship gets a notice saying which lockup holds you. You keep your ID, your implants and cyberware, and any ship key or mission item.

Your sentence clock runs whether you are awake, in critical condition, asleep or logged out. Wall boards in the yard and front room show the soonest release in `M:SS`; examine a board while serving time to read your own remaining clock.

## Life in the yard

You share the cell block with NPC prisoners, including [wanted criminals](bounty-hunting.md) other crews brought in alive, and two guards who walk rounds, drink coffee in the office and keep order.

- **Meals and clean clothes.** A galley behind the office puts fresh meals and clean prison jumpsuits onto the two serving hatches every minute. Dirty jumpsuits draw flies and sour the yard.
- **Passing the time.** Prisoners eat at the mess tables, shoot hoops, read, roll dice, play cards, complain about the food, keep grudges and remember players who shared a table, gave them a smoke or hit them.
- **Rumours and stashes.** Prisoners get mail on the serving hatches and hide things under mattresses or in toilet cisterns. Once per stay, a friendly prisoner may pass an inmate a tip about salvage or trouble out in the sector.
- **Fights and riots.** Guards answer unprovoked fights or weapons in the yard with stun batons, not guns. If someone attacks you first inside the cell block, fighting back in self-defence is allowed. When the yard turns ugly enough, prisoners bang the tables and start a **riot**, smashing doors and hatches to break out; high-tier bounty prisoners hit harder and stir riots up faster.
- **Medical care and death.** If you fall into critical condition inside the wing, an on-duty guard fetches the office first-aid kit to stabilize you while your clock keeps ticking. Dying in custody ends your sentence; a guard carries your body out through Processing to the front room and your ship is notified so crewmates can collect you and open your locker with your ID.

### Working time off

You can knock up to **one third** off your sentence by doing real jobs around the wing:

| Job | Time off |
|---|---|
| Mop mess that built up on the wing floor | 10 seconds |
| Wash a load of dirty prison jumpsuits in the yard machine | 30 seconds |
| Deliver a prisoner's letter from the hatch to the prisoner it names | 20 seconds |
| Dress or suture a wounded prisoner with supplies from the wing | 30 seconds |
| Hand stashed prisoner contraband to an on-duty guard | 60 seconds |

Making your own mess to mop or bringing outside items in earns nothing.

## Getting out

When your time runs out (or your 1–2 star fine clears), the board pings, a guard calls your name, and Processing cycles you out into the front room. Swipe yourself or your ID on your property locker to get your things back.

### Breaking out

Every cell door, staff airlock and serving hatch in the lockup is a real security door: you can hack or pry one with tools or an airlock override card, though a plain emag does nothing to it. Damaged or hacked lockup doors mend and re-secure themselves after 3 minutes.

- Walking out of the cell block and Processing on your own feet with time left is a **jailbreak**: at least 3 stars, and your old progress is wiped if they catch you again.
- Pulling, carrying or stuffing an inmate out charges the rescuer with jailbreak instead and gives the inmate **20 seconds** to walk back inside before they are marked escaped too.
- Loose NPC prisoners and break-ins bring outpost police up the elevator.

## Visiting and handing in bounties

Crewmates can ride up to the Lockup floor at any time to talk to an inmate through the front-room glass, wait with spare gear for their release, or pick up a dead crewmate and their property.

To **hand in a live criminal bounty**, bring the wanted target up the elevator to the front room while they are cuffed, stunned or in critical condition, and while the warden is standing at the front desk:

1. Pull the catch up to **Processing's outer door** and bump or click it. The outer door opens while the inner door stays shut.
2. Pull or walk the catch inside Processing, leave them on the floor, and **step back out** into the front room. Only you and your catch can step through the open outer door, and the warden waits until every player has stepped clear.
3. The outer door shuts, the bounty closes, and the warden calls out the payout.

Credits go straight to your crew's ship account (preferring a crew ship berthed at that outpost, or dropping a **holochip** at the desk if you have no ship). Trade vouchers, a **CAPTURED** poster on full-pay Wanted and Most Wanted catches, and any cuffs, space suit or other gear of yours on the criminal are set down on the counter spot in front of the desk.

- **Cuffed or stunned** catches pay **100%** plus vouchers.
- **Critical condition** pays **60%** in credits (with vouchers folded in at 1,200 cr each) and no poster.
- **Dead bodies** and **kill-only trophies** are refused at the lockup ("We don't take bodies."); turn those in on your ship's mission pad instead. See [Bounty Hunting](bounty-hunting.md#turning-in-a-catch).
