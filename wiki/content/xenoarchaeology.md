Some planet cliffs hold old dig sites: veined rock with something that isn't stone inside it. Most of it is small stuff such as fossils and pottery. Now and then a whole boulder comes away with an alien artifact in it. Getting one out in one piece takes care, finding out what it does means poking it, and a correct label is what the buyers pay for. A dug artifact can be sold at a [trader outpost](trader-outposts.md), bolted down aboard for its effect, or drained into batteries for a handheld projector.

## Where finds come from

Dig sites are on planet surfaces only. Ruins, asteroid fields and outposts have none. See [Planets](planets.md).

A site is a run of about four wall tiles in a cliff face, each threaded with pale veins. Two or three survey stakes with orange or red flagging tape and a heap of spoil stand on the ground in front, so a site shows from across the map. Examine a vein wall and it says pale veins run through the rock. A mining scanner marks the walls with a sign of its own, not an ore's. A world with no cliff to dig into gets a short outcrop of its own rock instead.

Mine a vein wall with a pickaxe or drill. Each wall gives up one find. It is either a boulder with a large find inside, or a strange rock with a small one. NPC miners leave these walls alone.

Every world holds three sites, and what is in them is fixed for the round.

| Zone | Large finds per world | Small finds per world |
|---|---|---|
| Green | 3 | 9 |
| Yellow | 3 | 9 |
| Red | 6 | 6 |

Sites do not refill when a planet unloads and comes back. A boulder you cut free but did not finish is put back as you left it, with its depth, tag and stabiliser field.

The zone the planet is in decides how good the finds are. Higher zones give rarer and stronger effects, better small finds and bigger payouts. Every effect can turn up in every zone, so a green-only crew can dig, study and sell everything. Freeing a large find in yellow or red can also wake whatever lives in the rock (see below). Only red space can hold a wrecked exosuit. See [The Overmap](overmap.md) for the zones.

## Digging

### Strange rocks

A strange rock is a lump of veined rock you can carry. It holds one small find, much shallower than a boulder's, 14 to 38 cm down.

1. Optionally sound it with a depth scanner. It reads a ten-centimetre window.
2. Chip it down with the hammer.
3. Finish with the brush.

No stabiliser is needed. Cut past the find and the find goes with the rock. A blast can destroy one too. When it comes free it drops into the hand that held the rock.

### Boulders

A boulder is the large find. It is anchored and heavy, and breaking it loses what is inside, so keep explosives away. Work it in this order.

1. **Tag it.** Use a mining scanner on the boulder. It gets a holotag with a catalogue number such as Kappa-4412 and shows on GPS units as that number plus "dig". Anyone with a GPS can pick it up, other crews included.
2. **Sound it.** Use a depth scanner. It pins a survey tag saying how deep the find sits as a 30 cm window, and how much has been cut so far. Finds sit between 25 and 200 cm down. The advanced scanner gives the exact depth.
3. **Learn the field.** Every boulder needs one of eight stabilising fields. The advanced depth scanner reads it on site. With the plain scanner, push the core sampler into the boulder (two seconds), take the core to a radiocarbon spectrometer aboard ship (about ten seconds), and read the "Matrix holds under" line on the report it prints. The report carries the boulder's catalogue number, so you can keep several apart.
4. **Stabilise it.** Use the field stabiliser in hand to pick the field, then sweep it over the boulder for five seconds. A wrong field looks exactly like a right one.
5. **Cut down to the find.** Mix tools as you like.
6. **Brush it clean.** Once the cut equals the depth, one more pass frees the find.

| Cutting tool | Takes off | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Pickaxe or drill | 18 to 30 cm | 2.5 seconds a swing |
| Rock hammer | The fitted head, 1 to 10 cm | Half a second per cm |
| Brush | 1 cm | 3 seconds a pass, 1 second with the quick brush |

A sounded boulder tells you the running total after every strike. The pick and hammer can overshoot. The brush cannot, so stop short and brush the rest. Walk away from a hammer swing halfway through and it may chip a centimetre or two off the boulder.

### Losing or cracking a find

- **Cut past the find** and the boulder splits along a fault. Nothing is left.
- **Free it without the right field** and half the time it slumps to pieces and nothing is left. The other half it comes out cracked.
- A cracked find sells for half. A cracked charged crystal holds half its charge and a cracked exosuit is in worse shape.
- **Smashing or blasting a boulder** loses the find.

In yellow or red space, freeing a large find can wake the local fauna: roughly one time in four in yellow and nearly one in two in red. The ground shudders, and about three seconds later a pack of animals or spiders crawls out four to seven tiles away.

## What can come out

| Find | What it is |
|---|---|
| Alien artifact | The usual large find. Comes in many shapes. One main effect, sometimes a milder second one. See Study and Using artifacts. |
| Bluespace formation | Pale blue crystal on black rock. It has no effect to study and no buyer wants it. Each time it takes a hit it teleports everyone within three tiles a short hop away and sets off a small EMP. Broken, it leaves 2 to 4 bluespace crystals, which traders buy (see [Mining & Salvage](mining.md)). |
| Charged crystal | An amber crystal holding a limited charge. It can run a ship's power for a while. |
| Sealed case | A sealed specimen transfer cache of lab and research gear, better the higher the zone it was dug in. It can hold a weapon. |
| Exosuit wreck | Very rare, red zone only. A Gygax or Durand, badly damaged, nearly flat on charge, caked in dust and carrying no weapons. Wash it, repair it and arm it yourselves. |
| Curios | Shells, plant fossils, bones, skulls, skeletons, bowls, urns, statuettes, instruments, idols, seed pods, corroded mechanisms and spent sidearms. They sell. |
| Research notes | A page worth 100 or 250 research points. |
| Strange object | A device of unknown purpose. Boffin buys them. |

Small finds come out of strange rocks. Large finds come out of boulders. Every artifact, curio and strange object is stamped with the zone it was dug in, and that stamp is what pricing and research read, wherever you carry it afterwards.

## Moving finds

A large find is dense and unbolted, so you pull it. Pulling or bumping an artifact counts as touching it, at most once every three seconds. That wakes a touch-triggered artifact unless you wear latex gloves.

!!! warning "Hauled artifacts can go off"
    Latex gloves stop you waking a touch-triggered artifact. They do not stop one that is already awake from acting on you.

A large find travels in a **specimen crate**, a padded, lead-lined crate built for one large find. Open the crate, then either drag the find onto it (a few seconds' heave, and the lid shuts on it) or push the find onto the open crate and close the lid. Ordinary crates will not take one, and a bolted-down find will not go in. The specimen crate also holds small things, never people or other machines, and its lid shows the find's catalogue number. While a find is shut in a crate it does nothing at all.

Every way into a trader outpost runs through a scan chamber. A loose artifact on the chamber floor makes the guard refuse the whole chamber ("Crate it first.") until someone takes it back out. A find in a shut crate passes. Going out is never a problem. Inside an outpost an artifact does nothing and cannot be bolted down. The lift between floors carries unbolted things standing in its alcove, so a crate rides up with you.

## Study

Large finds are read at an **artifact analyser** beside a **scan pad**. Both need power, must be bolted down and need their panels closed. Stand the find on the pad and open the analyser.

### Scanning

A scan takes about ten seconds. Better scanning modules shorten it. The analyser prints a report with the condition of the find and one line for each emission it has, giving three things:

- the **energy kind**, which also sets the colour of the glow when the find wakes;
- how it is released: on contact, as a field, or in bursts;
- what it reacts to: physical contact, energetic contact, or its surroundings.

A find with only one emission prints "None detected" for the secondary one, which tells you whether the second effect on a label should say None. The report never names the effect. The first completed scan of each dug find also prints research notes, once per find however many crews scan it: 100 points for a green find, 150 for yellow and 250 for red, and half as much again for the rarer effects. Take the notes to any R&D console.

| Report says | Glow |
|---|---|
| Concentrated energy | Amber |
| Psionic wavefront | Violet |
| Electromagnetic energy | Pale blue |
| High-frequency particles | White |
| Organically reactive particles | Rose red |
| Bluespace phasing | Deep blue |
| Atomic synthesis | Green |

Heat and cold report as energetic contact even though air temperature sets them off. Plasma, carbon dioxide and proximity report as surroundings.

### Testing

The report narrows an artifact down. To find the rest, apply stimuli to the find on the pad and watch the analyser's **tape**. Each stimulus prints a line such as "Water. Response." or "Touch. No response.", and each emission prints "emission rising" and "emission fading" as it wakes and sleeps. Air and proximity triggers print no stimulus line, only the rising and fading ones. The tape holds twelve lines. Tear it off for a paper slip.

Touch the find, splash it, hit it, burn it, shock it, change the air around it. The trigger table below says what each word means. Put on bio gear first (see Hazards).

A stimulus wakes any effect with that trigger for 45 to 90 seconds, and another stimulus restarts the clock. Air and proximity triggers keep the effect awake for as long as the condition lasts, plus a few seconds. An awake artifact glows, hums and gives off particles.

### Labelling

Open the **specimen labeler** in hand and pick three words from its lists: what the find does, what wakes it, and its second effect or None. Use it on the find to stick on the tag, which takes a second. Right-click peels a tag off. A new tag replaces the old one, so you can relabel as often as you like. Examining the find shows the tag as written.

Nothing in the game tells you whether the tag is right until a buyer pays for it.

## Using artifacts

### Bolted down

Anyone can bolt an artifact down with a wrench, or unbolt it, in four seconds. An artifact runs its effect wherever it stands, and bolting it down keeps it there. Some limits apply:

- A field uses its charge three times as fast as the artifact refills it, so it runs for about a third of the time and rests the rest. A burst fires when the store is full.
- No one can take more than 30 points of brute and burn healing from artifacts in five minutes, all artifacts and utilizers added together.
- A cell charger effect works on at most two ordinary power cells at once, at about charger speed. Machines and their cells are left alone.
- Gas effects vent only indoors, never onto planet ground or into space. Heat and cold change the air only indoors too, but they still warm or chill people anywhere.
- An artifact does nothing in a crate or inside a trader outpost.

A ship rebuilt from a [checkpoint](player-outposts.md#ship-bay) comes back without its artifacts, charged crystals, batteries or utilizers.

### Charged crystals

Bolt a charged crystal down on a cable and wind two lengths of cable around it, and it puts 15 kW on the wire. It runs until it has given 90 minutes of power in all, then goes dull for good. Cut the wire to stop it and keep what is left. A cracked crystal starts with half.

### Harvester, battery and utilizer

The **exotic particle harvester** draws one effect off an artifact into an **exotic battery**.

1. Seat an empty battery under the dome.
2. Put the artifact on a tile next to the harvester and wake the effect you want.
3. Press Draw. A full battery takes about 80 seconds, down to about 40 with the best capacitors.

Notes:

- Each artifact allows three harvests in all. A harvest is spent the moment charge starts to flow, and stopping early refunds nothing. Each one takes a fifth off the find's sale price.
- It will not draw while two effects are awake at once.
- An empty battery takes any effect. After that it tops up only from the effect it already holds. Purge a battery to reuse it.
- These effects cannot be drained: Twists flesh, Noise, Lightning, Moves on its own, Force walls, Shockwave, Vents plasma, Vents sleeping gas, Raises the dead and Fires spines.

The **exotic utilizer** is a handheld projector with a slot for the battery. Open it in hand to switch it on.

- A field or burst effect runs until the battery is empty, about six and a half minutes from full.
- A contact effect waits for you to press the prongs against someone, yourself included. A full battery gives ten uses.
- A field or burst reaches everyone in range, including the person holding it.
- It goes dark in a trader outpost.

### Smashing

An artifact is tough, but if it breaks, every effect that is not helpful lets off one last burst.

## Selling

Three outpost traders buy finds. Large finds sell only by hauling them to the trader.

| Buyer | Outpost | Takes |
|---|---|---|
| Barnaby | Waystation Halcyon, general store | Large finds, curios |
| Boffin | The Skunkworks, Quartermain Depot | Large finds, curios, strange objects |
| Vex | Undertow Exchange, black market | Large finds. Pays one trade voucher on top for an artifact with one of the rarest main effects. |

Sarge, at the Quartermain main counter, buys none and sends you round to Boffin. All three buyers pay the same for the same find. Selling earns no reputation.

### Selling a large find

1. Pull the find, or the shut crate holding it, to within three tiles of the trader.
2. Open Trade. The wanted ledger has a line for finds: "dig pieces" with Barnaby and Vex, "alien artifacts" with Boffin. Sell it.
3. The trader looks it over for a couple of seconds. Stay put. Walk away and the sale is off.
4. You get no quote and no second chance. The sale is final. Credits land on your ID's account, any voucher lands in your hand, and a paper receipt comes with them.

Each buyer takes three large finds before the next supply convoy, and Barnaby and Boffin take six curios. A convoy arrives about every 22 minutes and brings demand part of the way back.

### What it pays

Four things set the price of a large find:

- **The zone it was dug in**, not where you sell it. The base price is 250 credits for green, 450 for yellow and 700 for red.
- **Its main effect.** Rarer effects pay more, up to four times the base.
- **Your label.** The effect word is worth half of the price, the trigger word 30% and the second effect 20%. Add up what your label gets right. If it comes to half or more, you are paid that share of the price. Below half, or with no label, you are paid a fifth.
- **Condition.** A cracked find pays half. Each harvest takes off a fifth.

A perfectly labelled artifact pays between 250 and 1,000 credits from green space, 450 and 1,800 from yellow, and 700 and 2,800 from red, depending on its main effect. The best of those in red also earns a voucher at Vex. Vouchers are covered on [Money & Trade](money-and-trade.md).

### Curios

Barnaby and Boffin pay by the piece. Vex buys none.

| Credits | Items |
|---|---|
| 40 | Fossil shell |
| 50 | Plant fossil, fossil bone, fossil skull |
| 80 | Clay bowl, urn, corroded mechanism, spent sidearm |
| 150 | Statuette, strange instrument, idol, seed pod, fossil skeleton |
| 200 | Strange object (Boffin only) |

Only finds that came out of a dig site sell. A strange object from maintenance or an experimentor copy is not one. Feeding a dug curio or strange object to the destructive analyzer pays research points, once per kind per research server. Rarer finds pay more.

## Hazards and logging

- **Artifacts act on you.** A field or burst reaches everyone in range, and a contact effect hits whoever touches it. Bio suit and hood together stop most of an effect. Latex gloves and science goggles add the rest. Full protection also blocks the good effects.
- **Harmful effects are logged as attacks.** The person answering for it is whoever last woke, hit, splashed or hauled the artifact. For a utilizer it is whoever switched it on. A field logs each victim at most twice a minute. Nothing stops an artifact hurting another crew, and admins can see who did it.
- **Tagging, labelling, harvesting and selling are logged too,** and so is freeing a find that wakes an ambush.
- **Bolted artifacts act on your ship.** One that vents gas, irradiates or hurts people does it in your corridor.
