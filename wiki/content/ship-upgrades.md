The shipyard lets you choose a hull, theme and modules before launching. Purchases use [ship parts](ship-parts.md) banked to your account, and everything you buy stays unlocked for future rounds. For the hulls themselves, see the [Ship Catalog](ship-catalog.md).

## Ship parts

Parts come in four classes: **combat, science, trade and misc**. You need each class listed in a purchase's price; other classes cannot substitute. The shipyard displays your banked balance and each item's cost. See [Ship Parts](ship-parts.md) for loot sources, participation rewards and trading parts with other players.

### Getting parts home

Use **Request Extraction Case** under the **IC** tab, put up to five parts inside, and carry the case when your ship bluespace jumps or the round ends. A case inside your backpack counts; loose parts do not. You must be alive and connected, and the current carrier receives the parts. Each account extracts once per round, from one standard case plus any champion's cases carried alongside it. Read [Banking your parts](ship-parts.md#banking-your-parts) before leaving with a haul.

## The shipyard

From the lobby, the join menu has a **Start Your Own Ship** section with an **Open Shipyard** button. Everything about buying a ship happens in that one window, because it is the only place that shows you what you are buying.

It has three tabs beside the map preview. **Hull** is the shelf: every ship with its part cost, crew capacity, slot count and theme count. **Theme** lists that hull's whole-ship variants, with the free default pre-selected. **Upgrades** gives one row per slot and the modules that fit it, with one free default in each.

The preview panel draws the actual ship map, and it is live: change the theme and the whole hull redraws, pick a different module and that room changes in place. Use it. A hull's description tells you less than its floor plan does.

Buying anything asks for confirmation and tells you the cost. Purchases are one-time and permanent: a theme or module you unlock is yours on every future ship of that class. Selecting is separate from buying, so you can own four modules for a slot and still fly the default. Once the hull is paid for, **LAUNCH SHIP** appears next to Cancel.

!!! tip "Look before you spend"
    Switching hulls in the shipyard costs nothing and the preview works on hulls you do not own. Read every floor plan first.

## What happens when you launch

Your ship is built to order (hull, theme and the module in each slot) and placed in the safe inner ring of the overmap. You spawn aboard as its captain, so you set its course and manage its crew. It then appears in every other player's join menu with its job list, so people can pick a role and spawn straight in. If you want a crew, launch early and put something useful in the ship's memo. Then read [Piloting](piloting.md), [Ship Systems](ship-systems.md) and [Ship Combat](ship-combat.md).

## Modular hulls: slots and modules

A modular hull is a fixed frame with empty pockets in it. The frame owns everything the ship cannot fly without (helm, engines, power, air, airlocks and at least one cryopod) so a bare hull with every slot on its default module is a working ship. Slots are the rooms, not the plumbing.

Two rules follow:

- **Every module for a slot brings that slot's critical machines.** Every Goon engineering module includes a generator and a SMES; every Goon mining module includes an autolathe. You cannot fit yourself out of a power source or a fabricator by picking the wrong room.
- **Modules add, they do not subtract from the hull.** What a module drops is the *other* module's contents, not the ship's infrastructure. Swapping the Goon's Prospector Bay for the Freight Dock keeps the autolathe and loses the ore redemption machine.

Some modules also add a job to the manifest, a hydroponics bay brings a Botanist, a brig brings a security role. Those seats stack on top of the theme's crew list, so a fitout choice is a headcount choice too.

## Themes

A theme is a refit of the entire ship, not a paint job on one room. It changes the deck, the walls, the lighting and the fittings bow to stern, and it rewrites the crew list, job titles and starting outfits both. The Kilo's four are the Dust Hauler (free, the default), the Saloon (a timber refit with a piano hall in the cargo bay and a Barkeep on the manifest), the Dollhouse and Factory Fresh. The Phalanx's turn a marine carrier into Fleet Surplus, a spacer family's Hearthship, the garden ship Verdant, or The Springs, a travelling bathhouse with hot pools where the mess used to be.

Each hull's default theme is free; the rest cost parts, and you must own one before you can select it. Themes also decide which modules are on offer, so pick the theme first and the modules after.

## The round-start fleet

The ships the round begins with are rolled at random and cost nobody anything: a random hull, a random theme, and a random module in every slot. The fleet is sized to turnout (roughly one hull per six players who ready up, to a maximum of four) and hulls are drawn without repeats, so a three-ship round is three different classes. See the [New Player Guide](new-player-guide.md) for how to join one.
